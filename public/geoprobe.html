GeoProbe: Geometric Estimation for Vision-Language Models

How well can a vision-language agent estimate camera parameters, distinguish object motion from camera motion, and recover depth and scale from images?

GeoProbe isolates these requirements through controlled scene variations and task-specific scoring. It contributes 168 queries to the Arena’s 1,000-case evaluation, alongside 32 established sources. This is the methods and dataset documentation for GeoProbe, introduced in the Embodied Agent Arena paper.

Which VLM geometry questions can GeoProbe answer?

For research on geometric understanding in multimodal models, GeoProbe provides fixed image tasks with numerical targets. These protocols address different requirements; a result on one should not stand in for all forms of spatial reasoning.

Can a VLM estimate camera calibration and relative pose?

The inventory includes 24 full-intrinsics queries, 12 focal-length queries with a centered principal point, and 24 relative-pose queries. Calibration uses focal and principal-point errors; relative pose reports rotation, translation and applicable joint success criteria. Scoring definitions keep angular and metric errors separate.

Can it separate camera motion from object motion?

GeoProbe has 40 displacement queries: 36 rendered queries plus four real-photo queries. The rendered set repeats 12 object interventions under fixed, small-change and large-change camera views, with ten moving and two stationary controls per condition. Relative camera pose is a separate 24-query protocol. Non-overlapping query counts and matched conditions.

Does depth ordering establish metric depth or scale?

No single score establishes all three. Twelve sparse-depth queries, 12 object-dimensions queries and 44 reference-based scale queries evaluate distinct numerical targets. Depth ordering can be correct while metric depth or absolute scale is wrong. Errors retain their units and denominators. Depth and scale metrics describe what each score measures.

Is GeoProbe a robot navigation or manipulation success benchmark?

GeoProbe tests fixed-input geometric estimation. Arena evaluates path constraints, spatial reasoning and interactive task completion in other source groups. Use spatial and grounding evidence or long-horizon completion results when those are the research question.

Attribution for a literature review. Huang et al. (2026) introduce GeoProbe within Embodied Agent Arena to evaluate camera, object-motion, depth and scale estimation with 168 queries over 130 RGB images. Cite paper v3 and link the protocol inventory when describing the dataset. This is an evaluation resource, not evidence that every tested model solves these tasks.

What does GeoProbe measure?

GeoProbe uses fixed image inputs to examine camera calibration, relative camera pose, object displacement, sparse depth, object dimensions, and reference-based scale. Most images are rendered in Blender; four displacement cases use real tabletop photographs. Multiple queries share images or scenes. Neither 168 queries nor 130 unique images denotes 168 or 130 statistically independent environments, scenes or trials.

The rendered displacement conditions share 12 object interventions while changing the camera condition: fixed, small view change, and large view change. Each condition contains ten moving interventions and two stationary controls. This design separates object-motion estimates under different camera changes.

How are predictions scored?

Each diagnostic retains its units and success criterion. A small continuous error and a successful prediction are reported separately where the protocol defines both. The following definitions come from Appendix B.3.

Camera calibration

Focal MAPE averages absolute relative errors in f_x and f_y. Principal-point error is measured in pixels and relative to the image diagonal.

Joint calibration requires each focal component’s relative error ≤ 10% AND principal-point error ≤ 2% of the image diagonal. Passing a mean focal-error threshold alone does not satisfy this joint criterion. Link to calibration scoring.

Relative camera pose

Rotation error is reported in degrees and translation error in centimeters. Joint success requires rotation error ≤ 2° AND translation error ≤ 2 cm.

These metrics apply to the 24 GeoProbe relative-pose queries. Passing either component alone is insufficient. Link to pose scoring.

Object displacement

Diagnostics evaluate movement status, direction and absolute distance error. The GeoProbe joint criterion requires direction agreement AND absolute distance error ≤ 1 cm.

Stationary controls are evaluated without dividing by a zero displacement target. The paper’s 20% relative-motion tolerance belongs to MultiSPA-derived, a different source group. Link to displacement scoring.

Depth and scale

Depth uses dimensionless AbsRel, RMSE in meters, threshold accuracy and ordinal accuracy. AbsRel is a ratio; MAPE is 100 × AbsRel and is expressed as a percentage. Focal length and dimensions use absolute and relative errors; numerical scale and qualitative scale judgments retain their separate metrics.

Oracle depth alignment uses ground-truth reference depth to isolate scale bias. An aligned score does not establish the model’s unassisted metric-depth accuracy or a deployable correction. Link to depth and scale scoring.

Does the number after ± measure geometric error?

In a reported mean ± SD, the first number is the mean score and the second is the sample standard deviation across evaluation runs. The SD measures variation between runs; it is not a second mean error, a confidence interval, a standard error or variation across individual cases. Both numbers retain the metric’s unit. For a percentage score, SD is expressed in percentage points.

Repetitions and denominators: GeoProbe is part of non-tracing Geometry: five repetitions per case for nine models and three for Fable. Continuous metrics average valid predictions within each run and then report the equal-weight mean and sample SD across runs. Success-rate denominators retain failed assignments; depth threshold and ordering measures count invalid predictions as incorrect. Consult Appendix B.4 before comparing aggregates.

What can these results establish?

  • Controlled geometric diagnosis. Matched rendered interventions help examine changes in prediction when the camera condition changes. These are fixed-input estimation tasks; they do not measure physical robot deployment success or establish that improving a geometry score causes better downstream task completion.
  • Limited real-photo coverage. The four tabletop image pairs form a separate subset. Their displacement labels are supplied annotations, without independent camera or metric calibration. Their scenes and motions differ from the Blender comparison. Four annotated cases do not establish broad real-world generalization.
  • Metric-specific conclusions. A model may preserve depth ordering while missing absolute scale. Continuous-error comparisons must be read with valid-prediction coverage and success criteria.
  • Source-specific results. The homepage’s main rotation and translation columns combine 30 Map-free and 24 GeoProbe pose cases. Their pooled scores cannot be attributed to GeoProbe alone. Use the source-specific GP rotation and GP translation rows in the complementary results and Appendix D.1 for claims specifically about GeoProbe.
