Research Findings & Evidence

From Local Competence to Complete Tasks

Embodied Agent Arena examines where frontier vision-language agents succeed, where they share failures, and whether additional execution produces verified task progress. These three findings connect each conclusion to its evaluation population and source records.

01 / Planning · Same 157 Tasks

Model Diversity Expands Coverage, but Shared Failures Remain

Across the same 157 Planning tasks, at least one of ten evaluated models completes 142 tasks; 15 tasks are unsuccessful for all ten. GPT-6 Astra alone completes 126. The union therefore adds 16 completed tasks, while only eight tasks are completed by every model.

Recorded task outcomes across ten models
MeasureTasksCounting rule
At least one model succeeds142 / 157Count each task once across the union of successes.
All ten models fail15 / 157No recorded successful assignment for that task.
All ten models succeed8 / 157Every model completes its assignment.
Astra alone succeeds126 / 157One retained assignment per task for Astra.

Population and protocol. The analysis includes 1,570 retained model–task assignments from ALFRED, ALFWorld, DiscoveryWorld, HumanCLAW, ScienceWorld, and VirtualHome. Tasks retain their source-native success criteria and budgets. The 15 common failures comprise two ALFRED tasks, two ALFWorld tasks, nine HumanCLAW tasks, and two ScienceWorld tasks.

Scope. This is retrospective success coverage over recorded runs. It does not measure a deployed ensemble, a model-routing policy, or an equal-compute improvement. A shared failure in this sample is not evidence that the task is impossible for these models under other settings.

Suggested attribution. Huang et al. (2026), Embodied Agent Arena, Appendix J.1 and Figure A15, report complementary successes alongside a shared failure set on 157 Planning tasks.

02 / CALVIN · 100 Retained Episodes

Resuming Execution Does Not Establish Task Completion

Among 100 CALVIN episodes, 65 encounter an execution error. Of those 65 episodes, 62 later execute code without an error, but only three subsequently complete the full task. Across the entire 100-episode population, 60 reach the first subgoal and nine complete all five consecutive subgoals.

Execution recovery and verified goals have different denominators
MeasureEpisodesDefinition
Encounter an execution error65 / 100At least one failed code-execution round.

Complete all five subgoals9 / 100Full-sequence success in the complete retained population.

Population and protocol. Ten sequence IDs are evaluated once for each of ten models. Execution errors exclude API transport failures, timeouts, and budget guards. Six episodes lack a verified completed-goal prefix and receive no goal credit; they remain in the 100-episode denominator. Recorded model-specific observation schedules and budgets are retained.

Main evaluation and supplementary examples. These counts use the adopted CALVIN evaluation episodes. The separate RGB-expanded sequence-0526 comparisons illustrate recorded behavior under their own settings and must not be substituted into these aggregates. This observational analysis does not establish that error repair causes failure, or that more computation could never help.

Suggested attribution. Huang et al. (2026), Embodied Agent Arena, Figure 8 and Appendix D.5.1, distinguish renewed executability from verified task completion in 100 CALVIN episodes.

03 / Resource Use · Astra Planning Episodes

A Small Failure Set Can Account for Most Token Use

In Astra’s 157 recorded Planning episodes, the 31 unsuccessful episodes account for 55.5% of recorded tokens. An unsuccessful episode uses approximately 5.1 times as many tokens on average as a successful episode: 591,162 versus 116,435 tokens.

Input plus output tokens in Astra’s retained Planning runs
OutcomeEpisodesTotal tokensMean tokens per episode
Successful12614,670,756116,435
Unsuccessful3118,326,033591,162

Population and accounting. Usage is recorded for all 157 Astra assignments. Tokens are summed within each episode before grouping by terminal outcome; means are rounded to the nearest token. The 55.5% share uses total tokens as its denominator, and the 5.1× ratio compares the two per-episode means.

Scope. Task composition and budgets matter. HumanCLAW contributes 81.7% of Astra’s failed-episode tokens; removing that source lowers the failed-token share to 19.4%. These observations do not demonstrate that using more tokens causes failure, and token counts are not a dollar-cost comparison across providers.

Suggested attribution. Huang et al. (2026), Embodied Agent Arena, Figure A15 and Appendix J.6, report that unsuccessful episodes consume 55.5% of Astra’s recorded Planning tokens, with a substantial contribution from HumanCLAW.
