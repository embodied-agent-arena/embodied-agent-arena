GeoProbe: Geometric Estimation for Vision-Language Models

How well can a vision-language agent estimate camera parameters, distinguish object motion from camera motion, and recover depth and scale from images?

GeoProbe isolates these requirements through controlled scene variations and task-specific scoring. It contributes 168 queries to the Arena’s 1,000-case evaluation, alongside 32 established sources.

Which VLM geometry questions can GeoProbe answer?

For research on geometric understanding in multimodal models, GeoProbe provides fixed image tasks with numerical targets. These protocols address different requirements; a result on one should not stand in for all forms of spatial reasoning.

Can a VLM estimate camera calibration and relative pose?

The inventory includes 24 full-intrinsics queries, 12 focal-length queries with a centered principal point, and 24 relative-pose queries. Calibration uses focal and principal-point errors; relative pose reports rotation, translation and applicable joint success criteria. Scoring definitions keep angular and metric errors separate.

Can it separate camera motion from object motion?

Three rendered displacement conditions repeat 12 object interventions under fixed, small-change and large-change camera views. Each contains ten moving and two stationary controls. This supports comparisons across camera conditions; the four real-photo cases form a separate subset. Matched conditions and real-photo limits.

Does depth ordering establish metric depth or scale?

No single score establishes all three. Twelve sparse-depth queries and 44 reference-based scale queries evaluate distinct numerical targets. Depth error, relative error and scale error must retain their units and denominators. Depth and scale metrics describe what each score measures.

Is GeoProbe a robot navigation or manipulation success benchmark?

GeoProbe tests fixed-input geometric estimation. Arena evaluates path constraints, spatial reasoning and interactive task completion in other source groups. Use spatial and grounding evidence or long-horizon completion results when those are the research question.

What does GeoProbe measure?

GeoProbe uses fixed image inputs to examine camera calibration, relative camera pose, object displacement, sparse depth, object dimensions, and reference-based scale. Most images are rendered in Blender; four displacement cases use real tabletop photographs. Multiple queries share images or scenes, so the query count is not a count of independent environments.

The rendered displacement conditions share 12 object interventions while changing the camera condition: fixed, small view change, and large view change. Each condition contains ten moving interventions and two stationary controls. This design separates object-motion estimates under different camera changes.

How are predictions scored?

Each diagnostic retains its units and success criterion. A small continuous error and a successful prediction are reported separately where the protocol defines both. The following definitions come from Appendix B.3.

Camera calibration

Focal MAPE averages absolute relative errors in f_x and f_y. Principal-point error is measured in pixels and relative to the image diagonal.

Joint calibration requires both focal components within 10% relative error and principal-point error no larger than 2% of the image diagonal.

Camera pose

Rotation error is reported in degrees and translation error in centimeters. Joint success requires both errors within the 2° and 2 cm tolerances.

These metrics distinguish a close estimate of one component from a valid estimate of the complete relative pose.

Object displacement

Diagnostics evaluate movement status, direction and absolute distance error. The native joint criterion requires direction agreement and distance error at most 1 cm.

Stationary controls are evaluated without dividing by a zero displacement target.

Depth and scale

Depth uses AbsRel, RMSE in meters, threshold accuracy and ordinal accuracy. Focal length and dimensions use absolute and relative errors; numerical scale and qualitative scale judgments retain their separate metrics.

Oracle depth alignment uses reference depth to isolate scale bias. It is a diagnostic analysis, not an independently deployable correction.

Repetitions and denominators: GeoProbe is part of non-tracing Geometry: five repetitions per case for nine models and three for Fable. Continuous metrics average valid predictions within each run and then report the equal-weight mean and sample SD across runs. Success-rate denominators retain failed assignments; depth threshold and ordering measures count invalid predictions as incorrect. Consult Appendix B.4 before comparing aggregates.

What can these results establish?

  • Controlled geometric diagnosis. Matched rendered interventions help examine changes in prediction when the camera condition changes. These are fixed-input estimation tasks; they do not measure physical robot deployment success.
  • Limited real-photo coverage. The four tabletop image pairs form a separate subset. Their displacement labels are supplied annotations, without independent camera or metric calibration. Their scenes and motions differ from the Blender comparison.
  • Metric-specific conclusions. A model may preserve depth ordering while missing absolute scale. Continuous-error comparisons must be read with valid-prediction coverage and success criteria.
  • Source-specific results. The homepage’s main rotation and translation columns combine Map-free and GeoProbe pose cases. Use the GeoProbe metrics in the complementary results and Appendix D.1 for claims specifically about GeoProbe.
